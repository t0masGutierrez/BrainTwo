### temperature
- measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of body
![400](4%20Physics/Images/temperature.png)

---
### temperature formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
T
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### celsius
- freezing point equal $0^{\circ}C$
- boiling point equal $100^{\circ}C$

---
### celsius formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{C}=\frac{5}{9}(T_{F}-32)\\
T_{F}=\text{fahrenheit}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### fahrenheit
- freezing point equal $32^{\circ}F$
- boiling point equal $212^{\circ}F$

---
### fahrenheit formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{F}=(\frac{9}{5})T_{C}+32\\
T_{C}=\text{celsius}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### kelvin
- freezing point equal $273.15\ K$
- boiling point equal $373.15\ K$

---
### kelvin formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_K=T_{C}+273.15\\
T_{C}=\text{celsius}
\end{aligned}
$$

### zeroth law of thermodynamics
- zero net heat flow between systems sharing the same temperature
- aka thermal equilibrium
![400](4%20Physics/Images/zeroth%20law%20of%20thermodynamics.png)

---
### zeroth law of thermodynamics formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
(T_A=T_C)\land(T_B=T_C)\implies T_A=T_B\\
T=\text{temperature}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### thermal expansion
- solid expands as temperature increases
![200](4%20Physics/Images/thermal%20expansion.png)

---
### length thermal expansion formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Delta L}{L}=\alpha\Delta T\\
L=\text{length}\\
\alpha=\text{linear expansion coefficient}\\
T=\text{temperature}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### area thermal expansion formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Delta A}{A}=2\alpha\Delta T\\
A=\text{area}\\
\alpha=\text{linear expansion coefficient}\\
T=\text{temperature}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### volume thermal expansion formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Delta V}{V}=3\alpha\Delta T\\
V=\text{volume}\\
\alpha=\text{linear expansion coefficient}\\
T=\text{temperature}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### thermal stress
- compressive stress as result of prevented thermal expansion
![200](4%20Physics/Images/thermal%20stress.png)

---
### thermal stress formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{F}{A}=Y\alpha\Delta T\\
F=\text{force}\\
A=\text{area}\\
Y=\text{young's modulus}\\
\alpha=\text{linear expansion coefficient}\\
T=\text{temperature}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### heat
- transfer of thermal energy from high temperature to low temperature
![[4 Physics/Images/heat.png|300]]

---
### heat formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T\ne0\implies Q\ne0\\
T=\text{temperature}\\
Q=\text{heat}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### kilocalorie
- amount of heat required to change temperature of 1 kilogram of water by 1 degree celsius
![400](4%20Physics/Images/kilocalorie.png)

---
### kilocalorie formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
1\ \text{kcal}=4186\ \text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### specific heat
- amount of heat required to change the temperature of 1 kilogram of substance by 1 degree celsius without changing its phase

---
### specific heat formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=mc\Delta T\\
m=\text{mass}\\
c=\text{specific heat capacity}\\
T=\text{temperature}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### specific heat capacity
- high specific heat capacity resist temperature change

---
### specific heat capacity formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
c=(\frac{1}{m})(\frac{dQ}{dT})\\
m=\text{mass}\\
Q=\text{heat}\\
T=\text{temperature}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### molar heat capacity
- high molar heat capacity resist temperature change
![400](4%20Physics/Images/molar%20heat%20capacity.png)

---
### molar heat capacity formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
C=(\frac{1}{n})(\frac{dQ}{dT})=Mc\\
n=\text{number of moles}\\
Q=\text{heat}\\
T=\text{temperature}\\
M=\text{molar mass}\\
c=\text{specific heat capacity}
\end{aligned}
$$

### phase
- state of matter

---
### phase formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{solid}\\
\text{liquid}\\
\text{gas}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### phase change
- transition from one phase to another
![400](4%20Physics/Images/phase%20change.png)

---
### phase change formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\set{\text{solid, liquid, gas}}\xrightarrow{\pm Q}\set{\text{solid, liquid, gas}}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### melting point
- temperature where solids become liquids by adding heat

---
### melting point formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{solid}\xrightarrow{+Q}\text{liquid}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### freezing point
- temperature where liquids become solids by subtracting heat

---
### freezing point formula
$$
\text{liquid}\xrightarrow{-Q}\text{solid}
$$

---
### boiling point
- temperature where liquids become gases by adding heat

---
### boiling point formula
$$
\text{liquid}\xrightarrow{+Q}\text{gas}
$$

---
### condensing point
- temperature where gases becomes liquids by subtracting heat

---
### condensing point formula
$$
\text{gas}\xrightarrow{-Q}\text{liquid}
$$

---
### sublimation point
- temperature where solids becomes gases (without becoming liquids) by adding heat

---
### sublimation point formula
$$
\text{solid}\xrightarrow{+Q}\text{gas}
$$

---
### deposition point
- temperature where gases becomes solids (without becoming liquids) by subtracting heat

---
### deposition point formula
$$
\text{gas}\xrightarrow{-Q}\text{solid}
$$

---
### triple point
- point where all three phases coexist at thermal equilibrium
![300](4%20Physics/Images/triple%20point.png)

---
### triple point formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{solid}\rightleftharpoons\text{liquid}\rightleftharpoons\text{gas}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### critical point
- point where liquids and gases are indistinguishable

---
### critical point formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{liquid}=\text{gas}
\end{aligned}
$$

\end{aligned}
$$

$$

---
### conduction
- heat flow as result of physical contact
![200](4%20Physics/Images/conduction.png)

---
### conduction formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dQ}{dt}=kA\frac{\Delta T}{L}\\
Q=\text{heat}\\
t=\text{time}\\
k=\text{thermal conductivity}\\
A=\text{area}\\
T=\text{temperature}\\
L=\text{length}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### convection
- heat flow as result of fluid motion

---
### convection formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dQ}{dt}\propto A\\
\frac{dQ}{dt}\propto v\\
\frac{dQ}{dt}\propto(\Delta T)^{5/4}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### radiation
- heat flow as result of electromagnetic waves

---
### radiation formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dQ}{dt}=Ae\sigma T^4\\
A=\text{area}\\
e=\text{emissivity}\\
\sigma=5.67\times10^{-8}\\
T=\text{temperature}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### newtons law of cooling
- rate of heat loss directly proportional temperature difference between system and surrounding
![[4 Physics/Images/newtons law of cooling.png]]

---
### newtons formula of cooling
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dT}{dt}=-k\Delta T\\
k=\text{cooling constant}\\
T=\text{temperature}\\
\end{aligned}
$$

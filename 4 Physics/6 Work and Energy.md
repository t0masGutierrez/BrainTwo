### work
- transfer of mechanical energy by the component of force parallel displacement
![[4 Physics/Images/work.png|300]]

---
### constant work formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
W=\vec F\cdot\Delta\vec r=(\|\vec F\|)(\|\Delta\vec r\|)\cos(\theta)\\
\vec F=\text{force}\\
\vec r=\text{position}\\
\|\vec F\|,\|\Delta\vec r\|=\text{magnitude}\\
\theta=\text{direction}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### variable work formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
W=\int\vec F\cdot d\vec r\\
\vec F=\text{force}\\
\vec r=\text{position}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### work direction property
- force parallel displacement equal positive work
- force antiparallel displacement equal negative work
- force perpendicular displacement equal zero work
![[4 Physics/Images/work direction property.png|400]]

---
### work direction property formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
0^{\circ}\le\theta<90^{\circ}\implies W>0\\
90^{\circ}<\theta\le180^{\circ}\implies W<0\\
\theta=90^{\circ}\implies W=0
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### kinetic energy
- energy of motion
![[4 Physics/Images/kinetic energy.png|300]]

---
### kinetic energy formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
K=\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}\\
m=\text{mass}\\
v=\text{velocity}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### net work
- work done by net force equal change of kinetic energy

---
### net work formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
W_{\text{net}}=\Delta K\\
K=\text{kinetic energy}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### conservative force
- reversible work conserve mechanical energy
- dependent position and independent path
![[4 Physics/Images/conservative force.png|200]]

---
### conservative force formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
\vec F_{\text{cons}}=-\nabla U\\
U=\text{potential energy}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### nonconservative force example
- friction force
- drag force

---
### conservative force example
- weight force
- spring force
- electric force

---
### potential energy
- energy of position
![[4 Physics/Images/potential energy.png|400]]

---
### potential energy formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
\Delta U=-\int\vec F_{\text{cons}}\cdot d\vec r\\
\vec F=\text{force}\\
\vec r=\text{position}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### gravitational potential energy
- gravitational energy of position

---
### gravitational potential energy formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
U_{g}=mgy\\
m=\text{mass}\\
g=\text{gravity}\\
y=\text{position}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### elastic potential energy
- elastic energy of position

---
### elastic potential energy formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
U_{\text{el}}=\frac{1}{2}kx^{2}\\
k=\text{spring constant}\\
x=\text{position}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### mechanical energy
- kinetic energy and potential energy

---
### mechanical energy formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
E=K+U\\
K=\text{kinetic energy}\\
U=\text{potential energy}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### conservative work
- work done by conservative force equal negative change of potential energy

---
### conservative work formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
W_{\text{cons}}=-\Delta U\\
U=\text{potential energy}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### nonconservative work
- work done by nonconservative force equal change of mechanical energy

---
### nonconservative work formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
W_{\text{noncons}}=\Delta K+\Delta U\\
K=\text{kinetic energy}\\
U=\text{potential energy}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### conservation of energy
- if work done by conservative force then zero change of mechanical energy
![[4 Physics/Images/conservation of energy.png]]

---
### conservation of energy formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
W_{\text{noncons}}=0\implies\Delta E=0\\
W=\text{work}\\
U=\text{potential energy}\\
E=\text{mechanical energy}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### energy diagram
- mechanical energy as function of position
![[4 Physics/Images/energy diagram.png]]

---
### energy diagram formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
E(x)\\
E=\text{mechanical energy}\\
x=\text{position}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### turning point
- point on energy diagram where mechanical energy equal potential energy

---
### turning point formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
E=U\\
E=\text{mechanical energy}\\
U=\text{potential energy}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### equilibrium point
- point on energy diagram where force acting on system equal zero
![[4 Physics/Images/equilibrium point.png|400]]

---
### equilibrium point formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
F_{x}=0
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### stable equilibrium point
- local minimum of mechanical energy where displacement restore position

---
### stable equilibrium point formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
\frac{d^{2}E}{dx^{2}}>0\\
E=\text{mechanical energy}\\
x=\text{position}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### unstable equilibrium point
- local maximum of mechanical energy where displacement do not restore position

---
### unstable equilibrium point formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
\frac{d^{2}E}{dx^{2}}<0\\
E=\text{mechanical energy}\\
x=\text{position}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### average power
- rate of mechanical energy transfer

---
### average power formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
P_{\text{avg}}=\frac{W}{\Delta t}=\frac{\Delta E}{\Delta t}\\
W=\text{work}\\
t=\text{time}\\
E=\text{mechanical energy}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### instantaneous power
- rate of doing work

---
### instantaneous power formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
P=\vec F\cdot\vec v=(\|\vec F\|)(\|\vec v\|)\cos(\theta)\\
\vec F=\text{force}\\
\vec v=\text{velocity}\\
\|\vec F\|,\|\vec v\|=\text{magnitude}\\
\theta=\text{direction}
\end{lgathered}
$$

---
### efficiency
- percentage of power input conversion power output

---
### efficiency formula
$$
\begin{lgathered}
\varepsilon=\frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}}\times100\\
P_{\text{out}}=\text{output power}\\
P_{\text{in}}=\text{input power}
\end{lgathered}
$$

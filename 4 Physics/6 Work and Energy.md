### work
- transfer of mechanical energy by the component of force parallel displacement
![[4 Physics/Images/work.png|300]]

---
### constant work formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
W = \vec F \cdot \Delta \vec r = F \Delta r \cos ( \theta ) \\
\vec F = \text {force} \\
\vec r = \text {position} \\
\theta = \text {angle between vectors}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### variable work formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
W = \int \vec F \cdot d \vec r \\
\vec F = \text {force} \\
\vec r = \text {position}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### work direction property
- force parallel displacement equal positive work
- force antiparallel displacement equal negative work
- force perpendicular displacement equal zero work
![[4 Physics/Images/work direction property.png|400]]

---
### work direction property formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
0 ^ { \circ } \le \theta < 90 ^ { \circ } \implies W > 0 \\
90 ^ { \circ } < \theta \le 180 ^ { \circ } \implies W < 0 \\
\theta = 90 ^ { \circ } \implies W = 0
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### kinetic energy
- energy of motion
- or the amount of work the object can do while speeding up
- or the amount of work the object can do while slowing down
![[4 Physics/Images/kinetic energy.png|300]]

---
### kinetic energy formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
K = \frac { 1 } { 2 } m v ^ { 2 } \\
m = \text {mass} \\
v = \text {velocity}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### net work
- work done by net external force equal change of kinetic energy

---
### net work formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
W _ { net } = K _ { f } - K _ { i } \\
K = \text {kinetic energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### conservative force
- reversible work conserve mechanical energy
- dependent position and independent path
![[4 Physics/Images/conservative force.png|200]]

---
### conservative force formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec F _ { cons } = - \nabla U \\
U = \text {potential energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### nonconservative force example
- friction force
- drag force

---
### conservative force example
- weight force
- spring force
- electric force

---
### potential energy
- energy of position
![[4 Physics/Images/potential energy.png|400]]

---
### potential energy formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
U = \int \vec F _ { cons } \cdot d \vec r \\
\vec F = \text {force} \\
\vec r = \text {position}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### gravitational potential energy
- gravitational energy of position

---
### gravitational potential energy formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
U _ { g } = m g y \\
m = \text {mass} \\
g = \text {gravity} \\
y = \text {position}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### elastic potential energy
- elastic energy of position

---
### elastic potential energy formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
U _ { el } = \frac { 1 } { 2 } k x ^ { 2 } \\
k = \text {spring constant} \\
x = \text {displacement}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### mechanical energy
- kinetic energy and potential energy

---
### mechanical energy formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
E = K + U \\
K = \text {kinetic energy} \\
U = \text {potential energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### conservative work
- work done by conservative force equal negative change of potential energy

---
### conservative work formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
W _ { cons } = U _ { i } - U _ { f } \\
U = \text {potential energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### nonconservative work
- work done by nonconservative force equal nonzero change of mechanical energy

---
### nonconservative work formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
E _ { i } + W _ { noncons } = E _ { f } \\
E = \text {mechanical energy} \\
W = \text {work}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### conservation of energy
- work done by conservative force equal zero change of mechanical energy
![[4 Physics/Images/energy conservation.png]]

---
### conservation of energy formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
E _ { i } = E _ { f } \\
K _ { i } + U _ { i } = K _ { f } + U _ { f } \\
E = \text {mechanical energy} \\
K = \text {kinetic energy} \\
U = \text {potential energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### energy diagram
- mechanical energy as function of position
![[4 Physics/Images/energy diagram.png]]

---
### energy diagram formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
E ( x ) \\
E = \text {mechanical energy} \\
x = \text {position}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### turning point
- point on energy diagram where mechanical energy equal potential energy

---
### turning point formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
E = U \\
U = \text {potential energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### equilibrium point
- point on energy diagram where force acting on system equal zero
![[4 Physics/Images/equilibrium point.png|400]]

---
### equilibrium point formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
F _ { x } = 0
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### stable equilibrium point
- local minimum of mechanical energy where displacement restore position

---
### stable equilibrium point formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac { d ^ { 2 } E } { d x ^ { 2 } } > 0 \\
E = \text {mechanical energy} \\
x = \text {position}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### unstable equilibrium point
- local maximum of mechanical energy where displacement do not restore position

---
### unstable equilibrium point formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac { d ^ { 2 } E } { d x ^ { 2 } } < 0 \\
E = \text {mechanical energy} \\
x = \text {position}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### average power
- rate of mechanical energy transfer

---
### average power formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
P _ { avg } = \frac { W } { \Delta t } = \frac { \Delta E } { \Delta t } \\
W = \text {work} \\
t = \text {time} \\
E = \text {mechanical energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### instantaneous power
- rate of doing work

---
### instantaneous power formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
P = \vec F \cdot \vec v = F v \cos ( \theta ) \\
\vec F = \text {force} \\
\vec v = \text {velocity}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### efficiency
- percentage of power input conversion power output

---
### efficiency formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon = \frac { P _ { out } } { P _ { in } } \times 100 \\
P _ { out } = \text {output power} \\
P _ { in } = \text {input power}
\end{aligned}
$$

### work
- transfer of mechanical energy by the component of force parallel displacement
---
### constant work formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
W = \vec F \cdot  \Delta \vec r = F \Delta r \cos(\theta) \\
\vec F = \text{force} \\
\vec r = \text{position} \\
\theta = \text{angle between vectors}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### variable work formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
W = \int \vec F \cdot d \vec r \\
F = \text{force} \\
r = \text{position}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### work direction property
- force parallel displacement equal positive work
- force antiparallel displacement equal negative work
- force perpendicular displacement equal zero work
---
### work direction property formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
0^\circ \le \theta < 90^\circ \implies W > 0 \\
90^\circ < \theta \le 180^\circ \implies W < 0 \\
\theta = 90^\circ \implies W = 0
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### kinetic energy
- energy of motion
- or the amount of work the object can do while speeding up
- or the amount of work the object can do while slowing down
![300](8%20Physics/Images/kinetic%20energy.png)

---
### kinetic energy formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \\
m = \text{mass} \\
v = \text{velocity}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### net work
- work done by net force equal change of kinetic energy

---
### net work formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{net} = K_{f} - K_{i} \\
K = \text{kinetic energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### conservative force
- reversible work conserve mechanical energy
- dependent position and independent path
---
### conservative force formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec F_{cons} = -\nabla U \\
U = \text{potential energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### nonconservative force example
- friction force
- drag force

---
### conservative force example
- weight force
- spring force
- electric force

---
### potential energy
- energy of position
---
### potential energy formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
U = \int \vec F_{cons} \cdot d \vec r \\
\vec F = \text{force} \\
\vec r = \text{position}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### gravitational potential energy
- gravitational energy of position

---
### gravitational potential energy formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{g} = mgy \\
m = \text{mass} \\
g = \text{gravity} \\
y = \text{position}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### elastic potential energy
- elastic energy of position

---
### elastic potential energy formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{el} = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 \\
k = \text{spring constant} \\
x = \text{displacement}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### mechanical energy
- kinetic energy and potential energy

---
### mechanical energy formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
E = K + U \\
K = \text{kinetic energy} \\
U = \text{potential energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### conservative work
- work done by conservative force equal negative change of potential energy

---
### conservative work formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{cons} = U_{i} - U_{f} \\
U = \text{potential energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### nonconservative work
- work done by nonconservative force equal nonzero change of mechanical energy

---
### nonconservative work formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{i} + W_{noncons} = E_{f} \\
E = \text{mechanical energy} \\
W = \text{work}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### energy conservation
- work done by conservative force equal zero change of mechanical energy
![](8%20Physics/Images/energy%20conservation.png)

---
### energy conservation formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{i} = E_{f} \\
K_{i} + U_{i} = K_{f} + U_{f} \\
E = \text{mechanical energy} \\
K = \text{kinetic energy} \\
U = \text{potential energy}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### energy diagram
- mechanical energy as function of position
---
### turning point
- point where mechanical energy equal potential energy

---
### turning point formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
E = U \\
U = \text{potential energy} 
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### equilibrium point
- point where force acting on system equal zero
![400](8%20Physics/Images/equilibrium%20point.png)

---
### equilibrium point formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{x} = 0
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### stable equilibrium point
- local minimum of mechanical energy where displacements restore position

---
### stable equilibrium point formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^2E}{dx^2} > 0 \\
E = \text{mechanical energy} \\
x = \text{position}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### unstable equilibrium point
- local maximum of mechanical energy where displacements do not restore position

---
### unstable equilibrium point formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^2E}{dx^2} < 0 \\
E = \text{mechanical energy} \\
x = \text{position}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### average power
- rate of mechanical energy transfer

---
### average power formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{avg} = \frac{W}{\Delta t} = \frac{\Delta E}{\Delta t} \\
W = \text{work} \\
E = \text{mechanical energy} \\
t = \text{time}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### instantaneous power
- rate of doing work

---
### instantaneous power formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
P = \vec F \cdot \vec v = Fv\cos(\theta)\\
F = \text{force} \\
v = \text{velocity}
\end{aligned}
$$

---
### efficiency
- percentage of power input conversion power output

---
### efficiency formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon = \frac{P_{out}}{P_{in}} \times 100 \\
P_{out} = \text{output power} \\
P_{in} = \text{input power}
\end{aligned}
$$
